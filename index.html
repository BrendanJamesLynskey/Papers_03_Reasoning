The Five Papers

This deck traces the intellectual arc of LLM reasoning: each paper assumes the previous one and pushes the frontier of how much useful computation you can spend at test time. Read them in order and the rationale for o1-style and R1-style reasoning models becomes inevitable.

01

Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models

Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le, Zhou (Google) — 2022 — arXiv:2201.11903

The problem

Large models were strong at single-step pattern completion but failed badly at problems requiring several intermediate deductions — multi-step arithmetic word problems, commonsense chains, symbolic manipulation. Standard few-shot prompting gave a question and jumped straight to an answer, leaving the model no room to "show its working", and scaling parameters alone barely moved these benchmarks.

The contribution

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting: include a handful of few-shot exemplars whose answers are written out as intermediate reasoning steps before the final result. With nothing more than this prompt format change — no fine-tuning, no extra data — models decompose problems into steps. Critically, the gain is an emergent ability: it appears only above roughly 100B parameters and is flat or harmful for small models. PaLM 540B with CoT set a new state of the art on GSM8K.

Standard prompt

Q: Roger has 5 balls. He buys 2 cans of 3 balls each. How many now?

A: 11  often wrong

The model must compute the whole answer in one forward pass — no place to hold partial results.

Chain-of-thought prompt

Q: Roger has 5 balls. He buys 2 cans of 3 balls each. How many now?

A: Roger started with 5. Two cans of 3 balls is 6 balls. 5 + 6 = 11. The answer is 11. correct

Intermediate steps give the model serial computation budget.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

CoT is the cheapest reasoning intervention you have: a prompt-template change, zero training cost. Add Let's think step by step (zero-shot CoT) or a few worked exemplars before reaching for fine-tuning. But remember the scale dependency — on a 7B model CoT may not help and can hurt — and that a verbose chain costs output tokens and latency. CoT is the substrate every later technique in this deck builds on.

02

Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

Wang, Wei, Schuurmans, Le, Chi, Narang, Chowdhery, Zhou (Google) — 2022 — arXiv:2203.11171

The problem

Greedy CoT decodes a single reasoning path. If the model takes one wrong turn early, the whole chain — and the answer — is lost, even when the model "knows" the right answer along other paths. A single sample wastes the model's latent diversity.

The contribution

Self-consistency replaces greedy decoding with sample-and-marginalise: draw many diverse reasoning paths at non-zero temperature, then take a majority vote over the final answers, discarding the reasoning text itself. The intuition is that correct answers are reachable by many distinct valid chains, while errors are idiosyncratic and scatter. It is a pure decoding strategy — no training, no verifier — and delivers large gains over greedy CoT (e.g. +17.9 points on GSM8K with the same model).

Why it matters to a practising engineer

This is your first explicit test-time compute knob: spend more samples to buy accuracy. Gains are large at first and saturate, so tune N (often 5–40) against your latency and cost budget. It needs answers that can be normalised and compared for voting — perfect for math/multiple-choice, awkward for free-form text. Self-consistency is the conceptual seed of "sample many, then select", which Tree of Thoughts and PRM-based search generalise.

03

Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models

Yao, Yu, Zhao, Shafran, Griffiths, Cao, Narasimhan (Princeton & Google DeepMind) — 2023 — arXiv:2305.10601

The problem

CoT and self-consistency commit to left-to-right token generation: they cannot explore alternatives, look ahead, or backtrack from a dead end. For problems needing planning or search — Game of 24, creative writing, mini crosswords — a single (or even many parallel) linear chains fail because the model can never revise an early choice.

The contribution

Tree of Thoughts (ToT) generalises CoT into a deliberate search over a tree of partial solutions. It factors reasoning into four components: (1) thought decomposition into coherent intermediate steps; (2) a generator that proposes several next thoughts per state; (3) a state evaluator where the LLM scores or votes on how promising each partial solution is; and (4) a search algorithm — BFS or DFS — with lookahead and backtracking. On Game of 24, GPT-4 with CoT solved 4% of tasks; with ToT it solved 74%.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

ToT is the blueprint for agentic, search-based inference: it turns the LLM into both a proposer and a critic inside an explicit search loop you control. The cost is real — many LLM calls per node for generation and evaluation — so reserve it for hard, verifiable planning tasks where linear sampling plateaus. The generate/evaluate/search decomposition maps directly onto MCTS-style agents and the verifier-guided search of the next paper.

04

Let's Verify Step by Step

Lightman, Kosaraju, Burda, Edwards, Baker, Lee, Leike, Schulman, Sutskever, Cobbe (OpenAI) — 2023 — arXiv:2305.20050

The problem

To pick the best of many sampled solutions you need a verifier (reward model). The default is outcome supervision: label a full solution only by whether its final answer is correct. But a chain can reach the right answer through flawed steps, so outcome labels are a noisy, sparse signal — they reward "right for the wrong reasons" and give no guidance about where reasoning went wrong.

The contribution

The paper shows process supervision — providing feedback on every reasoning step — trains markedly more reliable verifiers than outcome supervision. Their process reward model (PRM) solves 78% of a representative MATH test subset by ranking many candidate solutions, beating an outcome-supervised reward model (ORM) and majority voting. They release PRM800K, ~800k step-level human correctness labels, and use the PRM to score/search over solutions.

Outcome RM (ORM)

One label for the whole solution: final answer right or wrong.

step 1 ... step 2 ... step k → final ✓

Sparse & noisy: rewards lucky-but-flawed chains; no signal on which step failed.

Process RM (PRM)

A correctness label at each step.

step 1 ✓ step 2 ✓ step 3 ✗

Dense & aligned: pinpoints the first error, so it ranks and searches far more reliably.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

This is how you make test-time search smart instead of just big: a PRM lets you score partial reasoning and guide best-of-N or tree search toward chains that are sound at every step, not merely lucky at the end. PRM800K is a reusable dataset for training your own step-level verifiers, and the dense reward is exactly the signal that makes verifier-guided search and RL on reasoning tractable — the bridge to R1.

05

DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

DeepSeek-AI — 2025 — arXiv:2501.12948

The problem

The previous papers add reasoning at inference time via prompting and search. The open question: can you train a model so that long, deliberate reasoning emerges as a native behaviour — matching o1-class reasoning — without an enormous human-annotated supervised reasoning corpus?

The contribution

R1-Zero: apply large-scale reinforcement learning (GRPO — Group Relative Policy Optimization) with simple rule-based rewards directly to a base model, with no supervised fine-tuning at all. The model spontaneously learns to generate long chains of thought, self-verify, reconsider, and produce "aha" moments — reasoning grown purely from RL incentives. R1 then fixes R1-Zero's readability and language-mixing issues with a multi-stage pipeline: a small cold-start SFT set, reasoning-oriented RL, rejection-sampled SFT, then a final RL stage. The result reaches o1-level reasoning and ships as open weights, with distillations into smaller dense models.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

R1 is the inflection point: reasoning is now a trainable capability with open weights you can self-host and distil, not just a prompt trick or a proprietary API. GRPO drops the value network, making RL cheaper to run, and rule-based rewards sidestep a learned reward model where answers are checkable. Practically: prefer R1-style models for hard math/code/logic; budget for their long thinking traces (latency and token cost); and note the distilled 1.5B–70B variants bring much of the gain to commodity hardware.

06

What to Take Away

  • Reasoning is bought with test-time compute. The whole arc is about spending more computation per query — longer chains, more samples, search, verification — and getting accuracy back.
  • Start with the cheapest lever. Chain-of-thought is a free prompt change, but it is an emergent ability — expect it only at sufficient scale.
  • Sample, then select. Self-consistency (majority vote) is the simplest test-time scaling knob; tune the number of samples against latency and cost.
  • Search beats linear chains on planning problems. Tree of Thoughts turns the LLM into proposer + evaluator inside an explicit BFS/DFS search with backtracking.
  • Verify the steps, not just the answer. Process reward models (Let's Verify Step by Step, PRM800K) give a dense signal that makes guided search and RL tractable.
  • Reasoning is now trainable and open. DeepSeek-R1 shows large-scale RL (GRPO) grows o1-class reasoning from a base model, with open weights and distillations.
